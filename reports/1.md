# Complete Methods and Materials Report for Manuscript Writing

Date: 2026-03-28
Project: ACS TNout early-warning framework (Ulsan main study, Seoul external validation in SI)

## Executive Summary

This report consolidates the full methodological record of what was actually implemented in the repository, with equations, optimization details, leakage controls, guarding policy, diagnostics, strict held-out evaluation, and reproducibility architecture. It is written as a reviewer-defense document so the main manuscript Methods and Materials section can be drafted directly from it.

The core methodological contribution is not only a model, but a strict decision-aligned protocol:

1. Leakage-safe forecasting across H = 1, 3, 5 days.
2. Operational evaluation at fixed alarm budget (top-k) rather than threshold-free score reporting only.
3. Guarded hybrid rank optimization that explicitly prevents conservative-budget regressions.
4. Full artifact traceability from code to fold-level predictions to final paper CSV/figures.

## A) Study Goal and Scientific Positioning

### A.1 Main objective

Build a leakage-safe, operationally meaningful TNout early-warning framework that answers the real plant question:

Given limited attention, how many exceedances are captured when only k alerts can be acted upon?

### A.2 Why this differs from common 2025-2026 trends

Most recent wastewater ML papers still emphasize global error minimization and post-hoc thresholding. This project is intentionally counterintuitive in three ways:

1. Decision utility is first-class, not secondary to MAE.
2. Conservative operating points are protected by explicit policy constraints, not only tuned for average improvement.
3. Validation is chronology-first and leakage-auditable at every transformation stage.

Every feature vector at time t is constructed only from information available up to t:

$$
\mathbf{x}_t = \phi(\mathcal{Z}_{\le t})

No variable from t+1 onward is permitted in \(\mathbf{x}_t\).

## C) Blocked Leakage-Safe Splitting and Validation

### C.1 Outer split: strict chronological blocked CV

All models are evaluated with expanding-window TimeSeriesSplit, n_splits = 3.

Fold i has train index set \(\mathcal{T}_i\) and test index set \(\mathcal{E}_i\), with strict ordering:

$$
\max(\mathcal{T}_i) < \min(\mathcal{E}_i)
$$

and expanding training windows:

$$
\mathcal{T}_1 \subset \mathcal{T}_2 \subset \mathcal{T}_3
$$

### C.2 Inner validation for deep training

Within each outer train block, chronological split is used:

$$
\mathcal{T}_i = \mathcal{T}_i^{\text{subtrain}} \cup \mathcal{T}_i^{\text{val}},\quad
\max(\mathcal{T}_i^{\text{subtrain}}) < \min(\mathcal{T}_i^{\text{val}})
$$

with val_frac = 0.15.

### C.3 Fold-safe preprocessing

Scaler is fit on subtrain only:

$$
\mu_i = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_i^{\text{subtrain}}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}_i^{\text{subtrain}}} \mathbf{x}_t,
\quad
\sigma_i = \mathrm{std}_{t\in\mathcal{T}_i^{\text{subtrain}}}(\mathbf{x}_t)
$$

and applied to validation/test:

$$
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_t = \frac{\mathbf{x}_t - \mu_i}{\sigma_i + 10^{-6}}
$$

### C.4 Why this is safest against reviewers

This protocol closes three common leakage channels:

1. Future contamination in feature engineering.
2. Global normalization leakage across folds.
3. Hyperparameter and threshold peeking on held-out periods.

## E) Optimization Objectives and Exact Equations

### E.1 Point forecast metrics

$$
\mathrm{MAE} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|y_j - \hat{y}_j|
$$

$$
\mathrm{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_j - \hat{y}_j)^2}
$$

$$
\mathrm{MASE}(m) = \frac{\frac{1}{n}\sum_j |y_j-\hat y_j|}{\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{t=m+1}^{n}|y_t-y_{t-m}| + \epsilon}
$$

### E.2 Alarm-budget decision layer

For budget r and sample size n:

$$
k = \lceil r n \rceil
$$

Scores are sorted descending and only top-k days are alarmed.

$$
\mathrm{Precision}@k = \frac{TP}{k},
\quad
\mathrm{Recall}@k = \frac{TP}{TP+FN}
$$

Random baseline expectation under top-k selection:

$$
\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Recall}@k] = r
$$

### E.3 BCR-TCN training objective

Composite training loss used:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} =
 w_{\text{reg}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{Huber}}
+ w_{\text{bce}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}
+ w_{\text{rank}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{pair}}
$$

with implemented weights:

- \(w_{\text{reg}}=1.0\)
- \(w_{\text{bce}}=0.6\)
- \(w_{\text{rank}}=1.4\)

Pairwise ranking component (softplus margin form):

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{pair}} =
\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}
\log\left(1+\exp\left(-(s^+_m - s^-_m)\right)\right)
$$

### E.4 Gradient clipping and stability

Raw gradient \(\mathbf{g}\) is clipped to threshold c = 1.0:

$$
\mathbf{g}_{\text{clip}} = \mathbf{g}\cdot
\min\left(1,\frac{c}{\|\mathbf{g}\|_2 + \epsilon}\right)
$$

### E.5 Hybrid rank score definition

Within each fold, component scores are rank-normalized to [0,1].

For threshold tau:

$$
S_{\tau}(t) = w_{\text{tcn}}\,R\left(p_{\tau}^{\text{tcn}}(t)\right)
+ \sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_{\text{reg}}} w_m\,R\left(\hat y_m(t)\right)
$$

with convex constraints:

$$
\sum_j w_j = 1,\quad w_j\ge 0
$$

### E.6 Guarding constraint (counterintuitive but critical)

For conservative budget points, candidate policy must satisfy precision floor:

$$
\mathrm{Precision}_{\text{candidate}}(\tau,r)
\ge
\mathrm{Precision}_{\text{paper-fixed}}(\tau,r)
$$

Optimization then maximizes recall subject to this floor.

### E.7 Guarded policy function

$$
S_{\tau}^{\text{guarded}}(t; r)=
\begin{cases}
S_{\tau}^{\text{paper-fixed}}(t), & r\le r_{\text{guard}}\\
S_{\tau}^{\text{policy}}(t; r), & r>r_{\text{guard}}
\end{cases}
$$

with \(r_{\text{guard}}=0.05\) in final guarded run.

## F) Exact Training, Validation, and Strict Held-Out Testing

### F.1 Training and validation

1. Linear and HGBR models trained fold-wise on chronological train blocks.
2. TCN trained fold-wise with inner chronological validation for early stopping.
3. Hybrid weights tuned using only training folds, then applied to held-out test fold.

### F.2 Strict held-out tests (two layers)

1. Outer blocked test folds (internal strict hold-out across time).
2. External strict hold-out site (Seoul) in SI without retuning for claim inflation.

### F.3 Why this satisfies strict review

This creates temporal and geographic hold-out stress tests, reducing optimism from local autocorrelation and site-specific adaptation.

## I) Methodological Innovations and Counterintuitive Novelties

### I.1 Innovation 1: decision-first evaluation

Counter to MAE-first trend, model ranking was judged by constrained top-k event capture. This directly matches operational staffing reality.

### I.2 Innovation 2: guarded optimization instead of unconstrained tuning

Policy optimization intentionally accepts not always maximal average recall if that would harm conservative operating points. This is a reliability-over-peak strategy.

### I.3 Innovation 3: dual-objective decomposition

Point and ranking objectives were optimized separately and then integrated, reducing objective conflict and improving interpretability.

### I.4 Innovation 4: strict leakage-safety as a design primitive

Leakage-safe split and transform discipline is not a post-hoc check; it is built into every stage.

## J) Ablation Logic and What Was Actually Tested

Formal ablation in this project appears as operational and component ablation rather than one single leave-one-feature-out experiment.

Implemented ablation dimensions:

1. Model family ablation:
   - persistence vs linear vs hgbr vs tcn vs hybrid
2. Horizon ablation:
   - H1 vs H3 vs H5
3. Operating point ablation:
   - tau and budget combinations
4. Policy ablation:
   - hybrid_paper_fixed vs hybrid_rank_v2 (guarded)
